# Section 6: NTL Derived GDP Growth Estimates in Somaliland

This paper offers the first attempt to validate GDP growth in Somaliland and aims to allow for subnational analysis of economic activity. As mentioned, there are several caveats associated with this approach. The outputs from the research should be considered rough estimates with large confidence intervals attached. The logged NTL radiance change approximates percentage growth for low growth rates, however this diverges for larger growth rates.

The first GDP statistics for Somaliland were published in 2012, real GDP (in 2017 prices) was $1,966m. Using the elasticities established in the literature review of 0.3 and 0.65, to estimate GDP results in a derived GDP growth that appears very different to that of the official statistics. 

This paper focuses on the change in the growth of real GDP of Somaliland. We do show the 2023 estimates of GDP to demonstrate the total scale of difference that there could be between official statistics and NTL derived estimates. However, this should not be taken as an attempt to ascertain the true GDP levels of Somaliland.

The period 2013-2016 shows moderate growth in NTL across Somaliland, however, post-2016 growth in NTL, faced rapid acceleration reaching a peak of 32.4% in 2021, before growth begins to slow. The average annual logged NTL radiance growth over the period was approximately 16.7%, substantially exceeding the growth implied by official GDP statistics. The divergence becomes particularly pronounced after 2016, when NTL growth accelerates while official GDP growth remains modest and in some years negative.

This is dependent upon the elasticity chosen. Utilising the lower bound for derived GDP growth statistics results in real GDP being approximately \$3,351m in 2023 compared to the official statistics of \$2,497m, which is around 36% higher. The upper provides a realistic (but high) estimate that GDP would be 159% higher than is currently estimated, at \$6,474m.

This provides evidence that Somaliland’s GDP statistics face issues and may not be fully capturing the economic expansion occurring in Somaliland. Under the lower-bound elasticity assumption, Somaliland's estimated GDP in 2023 would be approximately 36% higher than official estimates. The average annual growth rate implied by the lower-bound elasticity is approximately 5.0%, comparable to countries such as Kenya (4.6%) and Tanzania (5.4%) over the same period. The upper-bound elasticity implies average growth of approximately 10.8%, above even Ethiopia's growth of approximately 8.1% which is one of Africa’s fastest growing economies.

Secondly, this data demonstrates how the GDP of Somaliland is heavily concentrated in urban areas with very little development occurring in rural areas. Whilst this data will systematically underestimate pastoralist activities and farming the limited growth in NTL emissions outside urban areas suggests that economic expansion may have been concentrated in sectors and locations associated with electrification and urban development. The sobering implication from this is that significant shares of the GDP are concentrated within a few regions and cities. This suggests that the benefits of economic growth may have been unevenly distributed across regions and population groups.
